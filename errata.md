If we decide to do a reprint in the future, we will be using these to make corrections and improvements to the text, so feedback is appreciated!

* On Page 21: The Binomial Coefficient formula is flipped. It should be nCk = (n!) / (k! (n-k)!). The printed version flips the meaning of the standard interpretation of n and k from other materials.
  
* Page 22: The comment "such as pi, which will always denote half of the circumference of a circle.". is incorrect without more context. Pi represents the ratio between the circumference C and its diameter, D. That value isn't the same as half of the circumference which would be Half = C/2, with C = 2*pi*r.  I was thinking specifically about the polar notation of complex numbers ωeix. When x=π, the complex number eiπ evaluates to -1 (Euler's identity). So, when x=π, the polar form of the complex number becomes z=ωeiπ and eiπ simplifies to -1 so z=−ω . This means that a complex number represented in polar form with x=π is a real number with a magnitude of ω, but it points in the opposite direction (a half rotation around the unit circle) on the complex plane compared to when x=0.
